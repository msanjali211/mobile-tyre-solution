Why Your Car Pulls to One Side (and When It's the Tyres)

A car that drifts left or right on a straight, level road is telling you something. The cause is often the tyres: uneven pressures, uneven wear, or a tyre that has gone soft on one side. Before booking anything expensive, check the pressures on all four tyres — a difference of even 5 psi between sides can make the car pull.

If the pressures are right and it still pulls, look at the tread. Uneven wear across one tyre points to a tracking or alignment problem. Wear on the inner or outer edge only is a classic sign the wheels are out of alignment. That needs a garage with alignment equipment, not a new tyre — fitting a new tyre onto a car with bad tracking just wears the new one out the same way.

Vibration is a different symptom. A steering wheel that shakes at motorway speed usually means a wheel is out of balance. That is a quick fix: the wheel is rebalanced on a machine, which any 24/7 mobile tyre fitting service can do at your home.

The rule is simple: check pressures first, look at the tread second, and get the car checked if the problem stays. Tyres are the cheapest possible cause, and the most common one.
