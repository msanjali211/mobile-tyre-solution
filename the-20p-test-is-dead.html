The 20p Test Is Dead: Better Ways to Check Tread Depth

For years drivers were told to push a 20p coin into the tread — if the outer band is hidden, the tread is deeper than 1.6 mm and legal. It is a handy trick, but it only tells you whether the tyre is legal, not whether it is safe. A tyre at 1.6 mm is legal and still dangerous in the wet.

A better check is a tread depth gauge, which costs a few pounds and gives an exact reading. Check in three places across each tyre — inner, middle and outer — and on all four tyres. Below 3 mm, start planning a replacement. At 1.6 mm, it is illegal and you need one now.

Also check the age of the tyre. The DOT code on the sidewall ends in four digits: the week and year of manufacture. A tyre made in week 30 of 2021 reads 3021. Rubber hardens with age, and most manufacturers recommend replacing tyres after five or six years even if the tread looks fine.
